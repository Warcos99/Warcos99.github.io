A Theory of Tensions

A Methodolgy for Understanding Beliefs

1. Setting up the Problem

The world we inhabit is one that is icnreasingly polarized and politicized. One of the unfortunate side effects here is that it is becoming harder to keep our sights on what do believe. We can get into the weeds about the nature of truth and episemology, but my interests in developing this theory of tensions is to facilitate understanding the mechanisms by which we believe what we do, and how those beliefs interact with each other. Going through the forest of knowledge with epistemolfoy as your guide will certainly help you understand how it is that this forest came about, the composition of the soils and plants, it will elucidate why certain behaviors are present among the wildlife, but it will not help you find your way around and out of the forest into shelter.

Before we get too far here, I want to clearly and flatly express some truths that are not being called into question. The following statements are true in so far as there exists mountains of evidence to support them:

  1. Systemic racism is real, and it shows it's ugly head in many different forms.
  2. Queer people are marginalized and driven to dangerous situations for exercising their freedom to be.
  3. Patriarchical structures have pushed women down for generations.
  4. The Industrial Revolution and the advent of mass market manufacturing is putting a great strain ont he planet, from the creation of greenhouse gasses to the accumulation of too much trash for us to handle.
  5. Late stage capitalism is grinding the common man to dust for the benefit of the elite few.

If you have issues with any of these statements, you probably actually have an issue with the secondary conclusions one might draw from these statements (or you just refuse to see the truth). You might see the strain we are putting on the planet and draw one of several conclusions. Some popular examplse of conclusions others have drawn are:

  • We need to take better care of the planet in every respect, including personal responsibility to up-cycle and recycle.
  • we need to hold companies accountable for the mess they are creating, and we need to add legislature to control and fix how these companies pollute. Asking regular people to bear the weight of the problem is a tactic used by these companies to remove responsibility from themselves.
  • Relying on the governemnt to solve this problem is a fool's errand, we need to think of more radical solutions.
  • The Free market will address this problem in the way that is most efficient when it becomes a truly present porblem. We are not there yet

One last disclaimer here is that I do not plan on telling you what to believe. I have personal believes that are resulting from this theory of tensions, and as I explain the process I will use my beliefs as examples. But the intention is not to convince anyone of anything. I am not above propaganda as a tool for changing public opinion, but this is not meant for that. Instead I just intend to explain my system of characterizing all the noise around us into sensible strings of thoughts.

2. A taught string tied on both ends.

First, imagine a viewpoint that leaves no room for discussion. I will use the following example: Someone comes up to you and says "all items in category A are bad". When I suspect a statement like this is being made, I like to imagine it as a node in space. I tie a string to it and let the rope dangle. Then I think about the extremity of the statement and try to imagine it's opposite. One version of that for this particular example would be "all items in category A are good". I imagine that as a different node, and tie the other end of the string to it. The string will sit mostly taught between both nodes. Let's define a point that I will call the "apex" of the string. The apex will be defined as the point of the string that is feeling the highest tension between both nodes. Imagine zooming in on the apex, and seeing the neighboring points, points to the left of the apex will feel a strong tension force to the left node, and points to the right of the apex will feel a strong tension force to the right of the node. The point of the apex itself is feeling a net force of zero because the tension force on the left will exactly cancel out with the tension force on the right.

In this thought experiment, the string is probably obeying the laws of physics in your head, so the apex is probably smack in the middle. But imagine a more complex theoretical space with me. In this mind-space, each node comes with it's own "mass". Imagine these nodes exist in a zero-gravity environment, where the nodes themselves have mass and exert some gravitational field of their own. The result here would be that the rope would not simply apex in the midpoint of the nodes. The rope would flex and wane with respect to the gravitational field created by these two nodes. The apex of the string in this situation would be determined by houw large the gravitational potential of the two competing nodes would be. This apex is the point of "truth".

Importantly, the mass or weight of the nodes is determined by your underlying philosophies, and so the beliefs you accept as truths will be determined by those philosophies.

Let's do a quick example. The statement Violence is bad for societal progress is presented. So we compare that to a diametrically opposed statement. We come up with Violence is necessary for societal progress. Imagining both of those statements as nodes in space, we tie a string to both nodes. Depending on your personal believes, historical data, and any other relevant points of information you can start to imagine how the string is going to react when placed between those two nodes. For the sake of example, I will use my beliefs, but again, the point here is not to convince you to believe what I do.

What are the facotres that influence the weight of each node for me?

Violence is bad for societal progress

  • Guilt about hurting others
  • Fear of violence being done unto me
  • A strong desire to resolve conflict non-violently
  • Certain examples of successful non-violent social movements
  • Certain examples of violent movements failing

Violence is necessary for societal progress

  • Frustration with how slow change is supposed to happen in this country
  • fear of violence being done unto me by the state
  • realizing that non-violent methods are not as effective when the adversary can sell you ay movememt back to you for their own profit.
  • examples of non-violent movements have a backbone of violence (for every MLK Jr there is also a Malcom X)
  • The true monarchy killer ended up being the world wars.
  • A tendency to define violence very broadly. physical violence, rhetorical violence, etc... Any form of aggression can be called "violent"

Where does that leave things for me? Well it leaves the apex of the string somewhere in the middle, but favoring the "violence is necessary" side. What that ends up meaning for me is that certain types of violence are absolutely necessary (namely Rhetorical Violence) while other forms of violence a cannot condone (phsyically agressing other people). As such I do not believe I can ever be a pacifist, but I also think it will be really hard for me to ever injure someone else. This is a truth I have found for myself, and this truth may be different for you.

3. A note on complexity

One neat thing about this system of visualizing arguments is that it allows for a neat trick when considering more complex situations. On it's face, this technique does not seem much different than simply considering all your knowledge on a topic before making a decision about what your belief in that topic is. But it is easy to imagine situation where discerning your belief about a topic is determined by your belief in a another topic, and so on. In this situation, if one wishes to understand why they believe what they do, then they would have to go on this long winding road of understanding how different beliefs are dependently built on one another.

Visualizing your beliefs as these strings on nodes, it's easy to imagine what this means for dependent beliefs. A dependent belief is one that has it's own two nodes in mind-space, and whose string is tangled in with the one you are observing. From this viewpoint, you can imagine the placement of the nodes matters, as well as teh way in which the strings pull on each other. For example, in my brain, the nodes of extreme violence and extreme non-violence are on one axis, whilet he nodes of "make your art subtle" and "make your art not subtle" are on an orthogonal axis. Meaning that when I consider how the string of violence and the string of art are tangled together, I can imagine it as those two strings pulling on each other in directions not commensurate with their nodes, causing an incredible tension. That ends up being a true reflection of my feelings on violence and art. Art without teeth is bland, but art that is needlessly violent is provocative in ways that is not useful and often times masturbatory.

Realizing that your belief system is just a bunch of nodes and strings, and that each string is pulling on each other in interesting and unique ways is a layer of ocmplexity that is allowable by this frrame of mind, It makes parsing complicated relationships between already complicated ideas easier to understand.

4. A more grounded example

A lot of what I have outlined here is meant to help parse the complex questions of of the bigger world. Violence, Art, Truth, etc... This frame of mind is helful for understanding your place in that world, but itis also very handy for understanding an equally complicated field: social interactions.

I find that a lot of social tension stems from two parties believing something that is not commensurate with each other. A simple example, I may believe that I am fun to watch movies with, because I crack good jokes. My wife on the other hand my believe I am terrible to watch movies with because I don't shut up. (Don't worry, I don't actually talk during movies). If I am having a discussion (or god forbid, an argument) with her about this, I may have this node of "I'm worth listening to" in my head, clearly the opposite node would be "I am not worth listening to". So when she tells me "you talk to much during movies" I may at first hear "I do not want to listen to you". But modeling her statement in this network of nodes, I would imagine a string from "I'm worth listening to" to "I'm not worth listening to" as my perspective on the the matter. Then I would imagine a second string on a different pair of nodes titled "I talk to much during movies" and "I don't talk enough during movies" as her perspective on the matter. Through this process I would realize that our perspectives would not be aligned and I would be more receptive to listening and understanding what she is saying.

I may come to a realization that I may have to compromize my wise-cracking a little bit for her enjoyment. Or I the compromize might be that the move gets paused to allow for jokes without getting in the way of the movie, or something else.

Either way, putting the strings betweennodes, and evaluating the nodes, understanding where the strings get tangled and understanding where the truth lies in all of that is an important process in the journey of getting out of my own head, and existing in a space where people also exist.
